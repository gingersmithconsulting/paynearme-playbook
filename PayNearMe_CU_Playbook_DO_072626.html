Step 1 — Find Your Targets
🔍 NCUA.gov
Search by state → filter by assets $300M–$2B. Download the 5300 call report spreadsheet and filter by consumer loan concentration. Best source for accurate, current data on every CU in the country.
💼 LinkedIn
Search "Credit Union" + "VP Operations" / "Chief Lending Officer" / "COO" by geography. 500–5,000 employees as a proxy for asset size. Flag anyone who posted about digital payments, member experience, or efficiency in the last 90 days.
🏛️ State League Directories
Every state has a credit union league with a member directory. Free to access. Organized by asset size. Often includes key contacts. Start here for geographic territory building.
🎪 Industry Events
CUNA, NAFCU, GAC, state league conferences. Anyone who attended a modernization, lending, or operations session is a warm prospect. Post-event follow-up within 5 days is the warmest outreach you'll ever send.
🤝 Referrals
Ask at every 90-day KPI review: "Who else in your network should we be talking to?" Peer CUs in the same state or asset tier trust each other's word more than any vendor pitch. One referral is worth ten cold emails.
🔔 Google Alerts
Set alerts for "[State] credit union merger" · "credit union digital payments" · "credit union delinquency" · "[CU Name]". Trigger events surface in real time — you'll know before the prospect thinks to call you.

Step 2 — Qualify Before You Prospect

30 seconds. Three questions. If all three are yes — proceed. If not — park and move on. Don't spend prospecting time on CUs that won't convert.

The 30-Second ICP Gate
☐ 1
Assets between $300M and $2B?
Sweet spot is $500M–$1B. Under $300M = ROI story is harder. Over $2B = longer procurement cycles.
☐ 2
Consumer loans likely ≥20% of assets?
Auto, personal, mortgage mix. Check NCUA 5300. Low loan concentration = low call volume = weak ROI case.
☐ 3
Running Symitar, Fiserv DNA, or Corelation Keystone?
These are our confirmed integration paths. Other cores = flag for Solutions Consultant before investing prospecting time.
✅ All Three Yes → Proceed

Move to Step 3. This CU is worth your prospecting time and attention.

❌ Any No → Park and Move On

Add to a future pipeline list with a 6-month revisit date. Don't force a prospect that doesn't fit — it wastes your time and damages the relationship before it starts.

Step 3 — Research Your Contact

The goal is to know enough about their specific situation that your first outreach feels like you did your homework — not like a mass blast. CU executives talk to each other. A rep who shows up informed gets remembered for the right reason.

Which CU environment pressure is loudest? Growth & Relevancy · Margin & ROA · Delinquency & Risk · Engagement & Retention

Step 5 — Run the Slow Burn Sequence
The CU Prospecting Philosophy

Credit unions move deliberately. Pressure signals you don't understand their world. Every touch should add something — a data point, an insight, a peer story. Never follow up just to follow up. Earn the conversation before you ask for it.

Day 1
Email + LinkedIn
Send Email Touch 1
Trigger-based or insight-led. Open with their situation, not your product. One specific observation. One light question — not a meeting ask.
Send LinkedIn Connection
Same day as email. No pitch in the connection note — just a genuine connect. "I work with credit unions in your space and would love to stay connected."
Day 5
LinkedIn
LinkedIn Message — If Connected
Share something useful — the Payment Experience Gap report, a relevant industry stat, a peer story. Frame it as "thought this might be relevant to what you're working on." Still no meeting ask.
Not Connected Yet?
No action needed on Day 5. Wait for the connection to be accepted. Don't stack touches before they've acknowledged you.
Day 12
Email
Send Email Touch 2 — The Stat
Lead with their NCUA data — delinquency rate, asset growth, loan volume. Make it specific to them. "I pulled [CU Name]'s 5300 filing and wanted to share one number..." Light ask at the end.
Why Wait 12 Days?
CU leaders are busy. 12 days gives them room to breathe without forgetting you. This isn't urgency — it's respect for their calendar.
Day 18
Voicemail
Voicemail — 20 Seconds, One Stat
Keep it short. One stat. One question. No pitch. "Hi [Name], this is [Your name] from PayNearMe — I've sent a couple of emails about payment friction at [CU Name]. Quick question: is this on your radar for this year? Happy to share what we're seeing. I'll send a follow-up."
Then Send a Follow-Up Email
Within 2 hours of the voicemail — same question in writing. Short. "Just left you a voicemail — wanted to follow up in writing too. [One sentence on why it's relevant right now.]"
Day 25
Email
Send Email Touch 3 — Peer Reference
A similar CU story — same asset size, same core, similar pain. Frame it as "thought this might be relevant" not "here's our case study." CUs respond to peer proof far more than vendor claims.
Make It Personal
"A credit union similar to [CU Name] — [asset size], [core platform] — was seeing [specific problem]. After deploying PayNearMe, [specific result]. I think there's a parallel to what you're working through."
Day 35
Email · Final
Send Email Touch 4 — Warm Close
"I've reached out a few times — if the timing isn't right, I completely understand. I'll stop reaching out unless I hear from you. If things change, you know where to find me." Leave the door open. No pressure. No guilt.
Why This Tone?
CU executives remember how you made them feel. A graceful exit with no pressure often gets a response — or a call 6 months later when the timing is right. Burning the bridge gets nothing.
Next: Decision Gate →
Step 6 — Decision Gate
✅ Response Received
Move to Pre-Call prep immediately. Pull NCUA data, form your archetype hypothesis, pre-populate LeveragePoint. Show up to the first call already knowing their world.
🔄 No Response — Try a Different Contact
Wait 2 weeks after Touch 4, then try one level up (COO if you started with CLO) or one level down (VP Operations). Run the same 35-day sequence with a fresh angle. Maximum 2 contacts per CU before parking.
⏸️ No Response After 2 Contacts — Park It
Add to a 90-day revisit list. Set a Google Alert for this CU. Re-engage only when a genuine trigger surfaces — new NCUA data, exec hire, industry news. "Just checking in" is not a re-engagement strategy.
📅 Timing Issue / Out of Office
Note the exact re-engagement date they gave you. Set a calendar reminder. Send a brief email the day they said — not a reminder that you're following up, but something genuinely useful that happens to land on the right day.
The Long Game

CU sales cycles are measured in quarters, not weeks. The rep who shows up consistently with relevant insight — without pressure — is the one who gets the call when the timing is finally right. Plant seeds. Tend them patiently. The harvest comes.

The Only Job In This Meeting

Ask great questions and listen. Come prepared, not pitched. The rep who asks the best questions wins the deal.

In This Meeting
Open with: "Before I tell you anything about us — I'd love to understand how things work today. Mind if I ask a few questions?"
Identify the Environment (First 10 Minutes)
Growth & Relevancy: "How are you thinking about competing for younger members who've grown up with Venmo and Apple Pay? Where do you feel most exposed?"
Margin & ROA: "Is reducing operational cost on the board's radar? How are you scaling your loan portfolio without scaling headcount?"
Delinquency & Risk: "How much of your delinquency do you attribute to members who genuinely can't pay vs. members who just didn't get around to it?"
Engagement & Retention: "What % of your membership hasn't meaningfully interacted with you in the last 12 months?"
3-Layer Probing Sequence

It takes three questions to get to the real issue. L1 surfaces the symptom. L2 surfaces the business impact. L3 surfaces the root cause — the one they haven't said out loud yet.

📱 Growth & Relevancy ▼
L1"When a 28-year-old member wants to make a loan payment on a Sunday night — what does that experience look like for them today?"
L2"When that experience frustrates them, what does it cost you — in calls to your service team, in late payments, or in members you don't hear from again?"
L3"If younger members are quietly benchmarking you against Venmo and Apple Pay every time they make a payment — what does that tell you about where the relationship is headed?"
📊 Margin & ROA Pressure ▼
L1"Walk me through what a member experiences when they want to make a loan payment today — and tell me what percentage of those end up as a call to your service team."
L2"If each of those agent-assisted calls costs between $7 and $12 in staff time — have you quantified what that line item looks like annually against your loan portfolio?"
L3"What would it mean to your cost-to-income ratio if you could eliminate the majority of those calls — not deflect them, eliminate them — without adding headcount?"
⚠️ Delinquency & Risk ▼
L1"When a payment fails on your current platform — what does the next screen show a member? An alternative payment path, or an error message?"
L2"Of the members who show up in your 30-day past-due bucket — how many of those do you think tried to pay and hit friction versus members who simply couldn't afford to?"
L3"If friction is driving a meaningful portion of your delinquency — what's that worth to you annually in recovery costs, collection staff time, and loan loss provisions?"
🤝 Member Engagement & Retention ▼
L1"What % of your indirect loan members have had any interaction with the credit union beyond their auto-payment in the last 12 months?"
L2"When those members don't interact with you — what's your current strategy for re-engaging them before they refinance somewhere else?"
L3"If the payment moment is one of your only regular touchpoints with indirect members — what does it say about the relationship if that touchpoint feels impersonal and transactional?"
The Login Wall: Indirect borrowers often have no online banking credentials. If your payment portal requires registration, they hit a wall — and they either call in or they don't pay. Ask: "What does your guest checkout experience look like for a member who only has an auto loan with you and has never set up online banking?"
Share Thought Leadership Naturally
"We see across our CU clients that about 40% of delinquency is friction-driven — does that resonate with what you're seeing?" Let the data validate their pain. Don't lead with it.

Before walking the full demo, hand the prospect your phone or display a QR code and ask them to scan it on their own device. Land the emotional reaction before the feature walk.

Handle a Tough Objection
Every objection is a signal of interest — respond with curiosity, not defensiveness
The Mindset

The goal isn't to win the argument — it's to help the prospect see past their concern and back to the business case. Ask what's behind the objection before you answer it.

Common Objections & Responses
"We already have Fiserv / Jack Henry."▼
"Fiserv processes transactions. We manage the payment experience. Your core handles the banking rails — PayNearMe handles what members see, feel, and interact with when they actually make a payment. Most CUs running Fiserv or Jack Henry still have members calling in to pay loans — that's the gap we fill. We're an additive layer, not a replacement."
"We can't take on another integration project."▼
"Understood — and that's exactly why the integration path matters. We connect via Kinective or Core10 middleware, which means your IT team enables it — they don't build it. There's no core disruption. Our average go-live from integration kickoff is 6 to 8 weeks. Full member adoption ramps over the first 30 to 90 days post-launch."
"Our members are fine with how they pay."▼
"Is your portal buried 3 clicks deep? They're tolerating it — not fine with it. Check your delinquency data for the real answer. Members don't tell you the payment experience is broken — they just don't pay on time, or they call in instead of completing it themselves. The data tells the story they're not saying out loud."
"More payment channels means more fraud exposure."▼
"Fragmentation is the real fraud risk. Running multiple payment vendors with separate fraud tools and separate audit trails gives you less visibility, not more. PayNearMe is a single platform with built-in tokenization, unified channel visibility, PCI DSS 4.0 certification, and SOC 2 Type II. One platform means one place to see everything — and one place to respond."
"IT will push back on security and complexity."▼
"PCI DSS 4.0 and SOC 2 Type II — both available for review before the demo. We connect on top of your existing core, so there's no disruption to core systems. Our best client feedback from IT teams: 'The best part is I never hear from you.' That's what CIO/IT leaders actually want — a vendor that delivers and then disappears into the infrastructure. Share the IT Questions Guide with your champion before the scoping call so their IT team arrives prepared."
"That implementation fee is a big upfront hit."▼
"That's the most common budget objection we hear — and it's fair to name it. Two things worth putting next to that number: what are you currently spending on your existing payment infrastructure, including staff time for exceptions and call handling? And would it help to run the amortization math against your NCUA baseline? Most clients recover the implementation fee within the first 90 days through call deflection alone."
"Five years is a long commitment — can we do something shorter?"▼
"The concern is usually about flexibility — core conversion, digital banking switch, merger. Our integration sits on top of your core via Kinective or Core10, so a core conversion doesn't require starting over. What I'd ask you to weigh: CUs who see the strongest ROI are the ones who've had 18–24 months to fully build member habits on the platform. A shorter term may feel safer, but it limits how much of the upside you capture."

Note: Any non-standard contract term requests must go through your Sales Manager before responding to the prospect.
"A competitor is showing us something similar."▼
"Worth comparing — what specifically are they showing you? The differences come down to three things: payment type breadth (especially cash at 62,000+ retail locations), Smart Switch™ redundancy so you never have processing downtime at month-end, and our implementation track record at credit unions specifically. PayNearMe was named 2026 Best PayTech Solution by FinTech Futures and 2026 Top Payments ISV by the ETA. Happy to do a side-by-side."

Often the incumbent. Ask: "Is what SWBC is showing you a modernization of what you already have, or a genuinely new experience for your members?" Key gaps: no retail cash network, no Smart Switch™, built as a utility — not a member experience layer.

Publicly traded, broad market reach. Lead with depth of CU focus: "PayNearMe is purpose-built for the CU vertical — it's not one of many markets, it's our primary focus." No retail cash network, no Smart Switch™.

Competing for younger members in a fintech-saturated world

"How are you thinking about competing for younger members who've grown up with Venmo and Apple Pay? Where do you feel most exposed?"

Go Deeper
"When a 28-year-old member wants to make a loan payment on a Sunday night — what does that experience look like for them today?"

📊 Margin & ROA Pressure
Scaling the loan portfolio without scaling headcount
Identify the Environment
"Is reducing operational cost on the board's radar? How are you thinking about scaling your loan portfolio without scaling headcount?"
"What does your cost-per-payment look like for agent-assisted versus self-service today? Have you quantified that gap?"
Go Deeper
"Walk me through what a member experiences when they want to make a loan payment — what percentage end up as a call?"
"If each agent-assisted call costs $7–$12 in staff time — have you quantified what that looks like annually?"
"What would it mean to your cost-to-income ratio if you could eliminate the majority of those calls — not deflect them, eliminate them?"
⚠️ Delinquency & Risk
Friction-driven delinquency vs. true credit risk
Identify the Environment
"How much of your delinquency do you attribute to members who genuinely can't pay — versus members who meant to pay and didn't get around to it?"
"What does your payment abandonment rate look like? When a payment fails, does the next screen offer an alternative — or just an error message?"
Go Deeper
"Of the members in your 30-day past-due bucket — how many tried to pay and hit friction versus those who simply couldn't afford to?"
"Where are you seeing fraudulent payments — and what's the exposure across your current payment channels?"
"If friction is driving a meaningful portion of your delinquency — what's that worth annually in recovery costs, collection staff time, and loan loss provisions?"

Converting indirect borrowers into loyal members

Coach My Champion
The internal advocate who can't close without you behind them
Why This Matters

Your champion is selling PayNearMe internally while you're not in the room. If you haven't given them the right language, the right materials, and the right framing — they will improvise. And improvised pitches lose to the status quo every time.

Understand Your Champion First
What is their motivation? Career win, operational relief, board credibility, or peer recognition? Match your framing to what a win means to them personally.
What is their authority? Can they approve, or do they recommend? If they recommend — who approves, and what does that person care about most?
What objections will they face internally? IT complexity, implementation cost, board approval threshold, competing priorities. Know these before they walk in.
What's their communication style? Data-driven → give them the LeveragePoint model. Story-driven → give them a peer CU reference. Risk-averse → give them the evaluation framework.
The Champion Test

Ask: "If your CEO asked you tomorrow why PayNearMe — what would you say?" Their answer tells you exactly what they've internalized and what gaps you still need to fill.

What to Give Them
The Language Package

Their specific ROI number from LeveragePoint — built with their own NCUA data. Never give them a generic benchmark.
A peer reference: "A credit union similar to ours in size and core platform saw X." Peer proof carries more weight internally than any vendor claim.

LeveragePoint ROI model with their baseline NCUA data pre-populated
