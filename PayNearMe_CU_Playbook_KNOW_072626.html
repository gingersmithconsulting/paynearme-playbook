AE Mindset
The Three Principles That Don't Change

Before you read a single section below, internalize these. They are not rules — they are how you think about every deal, at every stage, regardless of what's happening in the pipeline.

01
Own every deal.
No excuses, no hand-offs without full context. You are the quarterback from first hello to implementation kickoff. Log every email, every call, every meeting in the CRM — if it's not logged, it didn't happen.
02
Qualify hard.
A bad deal in the pipeline is worse than no deal. A CU with no meaningful loan volume, no call center handling payment calls, or no business-side champion willing to own the outcome is not your prospect. Move on faster than you're comfortable with.
03
Ask more than you talk.
Every conversation should surface something specific about their world — call volume, delinquency rate, reconciliation pain, fraud exposure. The more you know about their specific situation, the more PayNearMe becomes their solution, not a solution. The rep who asks the best questions wins the deal. Pitches lose them.

Who to Target: The Ideal Credit Union Profile

PayNearMe's sweet spot is credit unions with active consumer loan portfolios and enough call center volume that payment modernization becomes a compelling, defensible business case. Smaller CUs may not have the volume to justify the ROI story. Larger CUs may have longer procurement cycles. The $500M–$1B range is where both business pain and decision speed align.

✅ Prioritize These Signals
  • → Assets: $300M–$2B (sweet spot $500M–$1B)
  • → Core: Jack Henry Symitar, Fiserv DNA, or Corelation Keystone
  • → Consumer loans ≥ 20% of total assets
  • → Call center / collections: 5+ FTEs handling loan payment calls
  • → Active delinquency challenge — friction-driven, not just credit risk
  • → Trigger events: new exec hire, merger activity, contract renewal window
⛔ Deprioritize
  • → Under $100M in assets
  • → No meaningful consumer loan portfolio
  • → Core conversion already underway
  • → No business-side champion willing to own the outcome
  • → No call center or collections team handling payment calls

Confirm with Chris Edson which configurations are in scope.

Buyer Archetypes — Read the Room Before You Pitch

Every prospect falls into one of three archetypes. Misread the archetype and your pitch lands wrong. Spend the first 10 minutes listening before you decide which play to run.

🟢 Change Agent
Signals: New to role (<18 months) · active on LinkedIn · aspirational language · "we need to modernize" · asks about what other CUs are doing
✦ Move fast · make them the hero · ask for CFO introduction early · a CEO/CFO/COO/CLO with 18 months left has inverted risk/reward
🟣 Reluctant Changer
Signals: Sees the problem but is risk-averse · "we tried this before" · asks about references before anything else · "what happens if it doesn't work?"
✦ Slow down · peer references first · reduce risk at every step · Heritage Acceptance as a dispute-handling proof point
⚫ Status Quo Protector
Signals: 8+ years tenure · operational language · deflects to IT · vendor fatigue · retirement horizon · "we've always done it this way"
✦ Don't pitch · find an ally above or below · show data they can't ignore · a retiring leader has inverted risk/reward on new initiatives
⚠️ Retirement Horizon Signal
A CEO, CFO, COO, or CLO with 18 months or fewer left has inverted risk/reward on new initiatives — the downside of a failed project lands in their tenure; the upside accrues to their successor. Don't pitch them directly. Find their successor now — the next leader who will inherit the problem and own the outcome. That person has every incentive to move.
2
The Status Quo as Competitor: The Cost of Doing Nothing

Your biggest competitor in this deal isn't SWBC or REPAY. It's the decision to do nothing. And unlike a competitor, the status quo never loses a demo, never misses a reference call, and never asks anyone to change anything. The credit unions most resistant to change aren't wrong to be cautious — they're wrong about what the risk actually is. The risk isn't moving. The risk is staying while the gap widens.

The Reframe

Most credit unions believe their core-provided payment portal is "good enough." It processes payments. Members can use it. Nothing is on fire. That belief is the deal killer — and it's your job to replace it with a more accurate picture of what "good enough" is actually costing them.

"What's it costing you to stay where you are?"

🔑 Three Types of Missed Payment — Know the Difference

Most CUs manage for one type of missed payment. PayNearMe addresses the one they're often blind to.

Members who can't pay → Hardship conversation. Credit risk. PayNearMe doesn't solve this.
Members who won't pay → Collections conversation. Behavior issue. PayNearMe doesn't solve this either.
Members who tried to pay but hit friction and gave up → This is PayNearMe's category. Most CUs have no idea how big this bucket is. Payment abandonment is the e-commerce cart abandonment of financial services — and most credit unions aren't even tracking it.

Six Ways the Status Quo is Losing Quietly
01 · Legacy Portal → Call Volume
The "Good Enough" Portal is a Call Center Funding Mechanism

A portal buried three clicks deep, designed for desktop, and lacking modern payment options doesn't eliminate calls — it generates them. Members who encounter friction don't abandon payments; they call in. Every agent-assisted payment costs $7–$12 in staff time (financial services average) for something that should cost a fraction of that.

PNM data: 17% call reduction in 30 days in a CU pilot. Up to 43% reduction in annual agent-assisted payments after deploying Smart Link™.

The Login Wall: Forcing members to register for online banking before making a loan payment is one of the single highest-friction moments in the payment experience. Indirect borrowers — who may have no relationship beyond the loan itself — are especially vulnerable. They don't know their account number, they don't have a login, and when they hit the wall, they either call in or they don't pay. Field research confirms this is one of the most viscerally felt pain points among digital and operations leaders in the CU space. Smart Link™ eliminates the Login Wall entirely — one link, one click, no credentials required.

"Stupid amount of calls for something so simple." — VP Operations, mid-sized CU (VOC)

02 · Delinquency as a Door-Opener
Most Friction-Driven Delinquency Isn't a Credit Risk Problem

The PayNearMe field team reviews delinquency rates from NCUA 5300 call reports before engaging a prospect — not to find CUs in crisis, but to show healthy CUs that their rate doesn't have to be what it is. A member who meant to pay, got busy, couldn't find the portal, and forgot — that's a payment experience problem, not a credit problem.

PNM data: When CUs re-engage members who abandoned a payment, 50% return to the payment flow and 7% complete the payment — meaning meaningful share of would-be 30-day past-dues never appear on a delinquency report. Industry benchmark: 5–15% lift in on-time payments across targeted portfolios.

Reframe: "Delinquencies don't have to be high for you. A meaningful portion of what you're managing as delinquency is actually friction — members who meant to pay. At your portfolio size, closing that gap is worth [X] in recovered balance annually, before we touch call center savings."

⚠️ Delinquency threshold: Verify with your Sales Manager which DQ rate makes this conversation most compelling before quoting a specific number in a prospect meeting.
03 · Payment Abandonment
The Metric Most CUs Aren't Tracking

Most CUs track delinquency. Almost none track payment abandonment — the rate at which members start the payment process, hit friction, and leave without completing. PNM platform data shows intelligent payment recovery turns approximately 9% of failed payments into completions, all within the self-service channel. That's revenue that was leaving silently.

Discovery question: "When a payment fails on your current platform, what does the next screen show? An error message, or an alternative payment path? And when a member walks away from a payment — how fast do you reach back out?"
04 · Member Dormancy
Dormant Members Are Relationships at Risk

The average CU has 15–20% of membership with no meaningful activity in the past 12 months. That's not just untapped revenue — it's a relationship at risk of being captured by a fintech or larger bank. A better payment experience is one of the most effective re-engagement tools available, because payments are the most frequent touchpoint a CU has with a member.

PNM data: 27% reduction in servicing calls after adding digital payoff flows. Smart Link™ enables proactive re-engagement — a personalized one-tap payment link delivered at the right moment to a member who's been drifting.
⚠️ Dormancy re-activation rate: Verify specific % with your Sales Manager before quoting in a prospect conversation.

Talk track: "What percentage of your members haven't meaningfully interacted with you in the last 12 months? What's your current strategy for re-engaging them? Because PayNearMe's Smart Link™ re-engagement flow turns a 'we haven't heard from you' moment into a completed payment — and it's fully automated."
05 · Reconciliation Pain
The Cost Nobody Talks About — Until You Ask

Reconciliation is one of the most consistently cited friction points in PayNearMe discovery calls — and credit unions almost never volunteer it until you ask. CUs running multiple payment vendors are managing separate files, exception reports, and audit trails daily. Four dimensions of reconciliation pain:

  • 1. File matching — daily manual matching of transactions across processors
  • 2. Exception handling — failed payments, returns, NSFs requiring manual research
  • 3. Reporting fragmentation — multiple systems, no single view
  • 4. Audit trail gaps — tracing errors is time-consuming and compliance-risky
"You'd need a drink to figure out our reconciliation." — CFO, mid-sized CU on GL complexity from multiple payment vendors (VOC)

PNM data: 89% reduction in dispute-handling time (Heritage Acceptance). 20–30% faster month-end close when exceptions are prevented, not reworked. $44K/month in overpayment prevention via automated Business Rules.

Talk track: "How many payment vendors are you currently working with? Walk me through what your reconciliation process looks like at end of day. How many exceptions do you handle per week — and who owns that work?"

Back-Office Teams Are Drowning in Manual Exceptions
"Payments hell." — COO describing their fragmented payment and back-office state (VOC)

The hidden cost of a fragmented payment ecosystem isn't just in the call center — it's in the back office. Field discovery consistently surfaces CUs manually processing thousands of exception items per year: skip-a-pay requests handled via PDF, NSF reversals requiring daily GL reconciliation, lockbox services for cash processing, and settlement delays that compound reconciliation complexity.

VOC benchmarks from field discovery (anonymized): A mid-sized CU manually processed thousands of skip-a-pay requests during a single promotional period — at roughly 7 minutes each, requiring multiple FTEs. A third was spending seven figures annually on a lockbox service for cash processing that a modern cash retail network would eliminate.

Talk track: "Walk me through what happens when a member wants to skip a payment or change their due date today. Is that a digital workflow or does it touch a person? How many of those do you process per month — and how long does each one take?"
